02 / Healthcare AI · UX/CX · Product Exploration

Healthcare, understood in your language.

Sahayak AI explores a more accessible healthcare experience for patients in India's Tier 2 and Tier 3 contexts—helping people understand health information, manage everyday care, and identify the next step with greater confidence.

The question behind the product

Healthcare should be easier to understand, wherever you live.

What if managing your health didn't depend on decoding medical language, navigating multiple tools, or having a family member explain every step?

01 / The challenge

Healthcare information exists. Understanding it is another challenge.

For patients managing chronic conditions, healthcare can extend far beyond a consultation. Interpreting reports, remembering medication, coordinating appointments, and knowing what to do next can create ongoing uncertainty—especially when language, digital familiarity, or access to continuous support becomes a barrier.

01 / Comprehension

Medical information can feel complex.

Dense reports, unfamiliar terminology, and difficult-to-interpret prescriptions can make treatment instructions hard to act on.

02 / Continuity

Care is spread across touchpoints.

Appointments, prescriptions, diagnostics, and follow-up instructions may feel disconnected from everyday routines.

03 / Coordination

Families often fill the gaps.

Caregivers help interpret information and manage reminders, adding emotional and logistical responsibilities.

How might we make healthcare information easier to understand and everyday treatment easier to manage?

04 / Research insights

Five barriers between receiving healthcare and managing it confidently.

The research pointed towards a need to bridge professional medical information and the everyday decisions patients make outside clinical settings.

01

Fragmented journeys

Patients may need to navigate appointments, prescriptions, diagnostics, and follow-up independently.

02

Limited treatment understanding

Complex terminology and dense reports can leave patients unsure what a diagnosis or treatment means.

03

High caregiver dependency

Family members often translate medical instructions into everyday actions and reminders.

04

Unverified information seeking

Uncertainty can lead people to online searches or social media for answers that may not be reliable.

05

Limited continuous guidance

Patients may need more structured support after leaving a consultation.

The opportunity

Connect information with action.

Design a healthcare companion that helps people understand information and identify an appropriate next step.

05 / UX strategy

Design for understanding, not just interaction.

The experience should not assume that users understand a feature simply because it exists. The strategy focused on making essential tasks understandable, predictable, and accessible.

01 / Clarity

Clarity over complexity

Use familiar language, clear hierarchy, and concise explanations.

02 / Guidance

Guided, not overwhelming

Break complex tasks into manageable steps with one clear action at a time.

03 / Access

Voice as an alternative

Explore voice-led interactions for people who prefer speaking or find long text difficult.

04 / Trust

Trust through transparency

Communicate AI limitations and encourage professional guidance when needed.

05 / Accessibility

Accessible by default

Use readable typography, large touch targets, recognizable icons, and consistent navigation.

06 / Continuity

Care beyond the screen

Consider patients, caregivers, and healthcare professionals across the wider care journey.

06 / Product concept

Your health companion, in a language you understand.

Sahayak AI brings essential health tasks into a more approachable mobile experience, with a focus on multilingual assistance and chronic-care management.

01 / Understand

02 / Manage

03 / Connect

Care coordination

AI assistance

Sahayak AI is a product concept intended to support understanding and care management, not diagnose conditions or replace qualified healthcare professionals.

07 / Information architecture

Organize healthcare around what people need to do.

The initial architecture explored a broad healthcare journey. The evolving direction focused on making the most important everyday actions easier to find.

Home

What needs my attention today?

Daily medication tasks, health status, quick readings, appointments, and relevant reminders.

My Care

How do I manage my treatment?

Treatment schedules, medication routines, lifestyle guidance, and progress tracking.

Health Records

How can I understand my history?

Reports, prescriptions, historical readings, simplified summaries, and record-sharing options.

Sahayak AI

Where can I get help?

Conversational questions, simpler explanations, and guided options for next steps.

08 / Voice-first UX

When healthcare language feels unfamiliar, start with a conversation.

The prototype explores voice as an alternative route to information—not as a replacement for clear text, visible controls, or user choice.

Speak

Express a question or concern through a voice-oriented interface.

Understand

Receive a simpler explanation in a more accessible conversational format.

Choose

Review clear options for an appropriate next step.

Continue

Move towards relevant care activities or professional guidance.

Technology should adapt to the user—not require the user to adapt to technology.
09 / Visual design system

Familiar, reassuring, and easy to navigate.

The visual direction uses restrained colour, structured cards, and clear hierarchy to make everyday healthcare tasks feel less intimidating.

Calm visual language

Warm off-white surfaces and green accents create a restrained, supportive environment.

Readable hierarchy

Clear headings, concise content, and separated sections support scanning.

Large, accessible actions

Prominent controls and generous touch targets make key interactions easier to locate.

Consistent components

Reusable cards and predictable patterns reduce the need to relearn interactions.

Visual health summaries

Charts and status indicators explore alternatives to dense medical data.

Ethical interaction

Supportive reminders, transparent AI limitations, and user choice around sharing.

10 / Iteration one

A comprehensive solution can still feel complicated.

The first design iteration explored multiple healthcare workflows. Usability evaluation revealed where the breadth of the experience introduced unnecessary friction.

What worked
  • +

  • +
    Users responded positively to AI assistance for everyday questions.
  • +
    Local-language support encouraged interaction.
  • +
    Guided steps made routine care tasks feel more manageable.
What needed improvement
  • !
    Too many onboarding steps.
  • !
    Layered navigation and overlapping feature groups.

  • !

  • !
    Health visualizations that needed clearer explanations.

14 / Product vision

From fragmented healthcare tasks to connected, continuous care.

The longer-term vision explores how a healthcare companion could support patients, families, and providers through more coordinated care.

01 / Expand

Chronic-care support

Explore future support for conditions such as hypertension, cardiovascular diseases, and kidney disorders.

02 / Integrate

Healthcare ecosystem

Investigate potential connections with providers, diagnostic services, pharmacies, and relevant digital health infrastructure.

03 / Personalize

Ongoing guidance

Explore adaptive education and lifestyle support aligned with individual care plans and professional guidance.

These are future product opportunities, not capabilities validated by the current prototype.

16 / Key learnings

Accessibility is not an additional feature. It shapes the entire experience.

The project reinforced the importance of designing around real user context rather than assuming every user approaches healthcare and technology in the same way.

Understanding is part of access

Medical information becomes more useful when people can understand it and identify an appropriate next step.

Design for real digital behaviour

Familiarity with everyday apps does not automatically mean confidence with healthcare-specific workflows.

Voice should complement visual UX

Multilingual voice interaction can provide an alternative path while clear text and visible controls remain important.

Caregivers are part of the ecosystem

Support family coordination while preserving patient choice and appropriate control.

Trust requires responsible AI

Prioritize clear explanations, transparent limitations, privacy, and professional guidance.

Testing changes direction

Usability evaluation exposed friction that visual polish alone could not solve.

17 / Closing reflection

Healthcare should leave people feeling more informed, not more overwhelmed.

Sahayak AI began with a question about the gap between receiving healthcare information and knowing what to do with it. The exploration moved towards a more accessible, task-oriented, and conversational experience—one that connects medical information with everyday action and recognizes trust as a core part of healthcare UX.
